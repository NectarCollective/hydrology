Watersheds

Every drop of rain belongs to a watershed.

A watershed (also called a drainage basin or catchment) is all the land that drains to the same outlet: a stream, river, lake, or the ocean.

How to spot one

  1. Ridges are the borders. The high ground between two valleys is the divide. Rain on one side flows one way, and rain on the other side flows the other way.
  2. The outlet is the drain. Everything inside the borders eventually funnels to one low point.
  3. They nest. A small creek's watershed sits inside a bigger river's watershed, which sits inside an even bigger one.

What shapes the flow

SlopeSteep land sheds water fast. Flat land holds it longer.
SoilSand soaks water up. Clay and rock send it running off.
CoverForests and wetlands slow water down like a sponge. Pavement and roofs speed it up.

Size and shape. Big, round basins collect water from everywhere at once, which can mean sharper flood peaks.

Why it matters

Whatever happens on the land ends up in the water: fertilizer from farms, oil from streets, sediment from construction. That's why watersheds are the natural unit for managing floods, drinking water, and pollution, and why a city upstream can change a river for everyone downstream.

Try it: Look up your address on the USGS StreamStats map to see the watershed you live in.
